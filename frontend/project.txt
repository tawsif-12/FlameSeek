Project Overview: The Harmonized Burning Activity Calendar & Predictive Intelligence SystemThis project is a multi-decadal satellite harmonization engine and spatial-temporal forecasting platform. It reconciles over 25 years of split Earth-observation data—specifically NASA/USGS MODIS (2000–present), NASA/NOAA VIIRS (2012–present), and JMA Himawari geostationary observations (2015–present)—with retrospective natural language processing (NLP) of historical media archives.By resolving sensor resolution disparities, spatial footprint mismatches, and diurnal observation gaps, the platform constructs an interactive Burning Activity Calendar. Land managers, climate researchers, and emergency response planners can use this calendar to query any Area of Interest (AOI), analyze true baseline fire regimes, identify historical anomalies, and run predictive risk models to anticipate future fire probability windows.System Architecture: How the Project RunsThe platform operates across four coordinated pipeline stages:[ NASA FIRMS Archives ]      [ JMA Himawari Archives ]      [ Digitized News Archives ]

1. Ingestion and Sensor Calibration PipelineSpatial Harmonization (Clustering): VIIRS has a 375 m spatial resolution at nadir, whereas MODIS has a 1 km resolution. A single physical fire front yields 1 MODIS active pixel but anywhere from 3 to 9 VIIRS pixels. The ingestion engine applies density-based spatial clustering (such as Spatio-Temporal DBSCAN) to aggregate contiguous VIIRS pixels into unified fire event clusters, matching the MODIS observational footprint.Overlapping Era Standardization (2012–Present): Using the ~14-year concurrent operational window of MODIS and VIIRS, the pipeline computes empirical conversion coefficients across different biomes (croplands, peatlands, tropical moist forests, savanna). This normalizes raw historical MODIS fire pixel counts (2000–2011) to VIIRS-equivalent units, ensuring consistent 25-year time series.2. Temporal & Diurnal Refinement (Himawari Layer)Gap-Filling: Polar-orbiting satellites capture only two daytime and two nighttime views per 24 hours. The high-cadence Himawari geostationary sensor (capturing frames every 10 minutes) reconstructs the full diurnal burning curve.Thermal False-Positive Elimination: Rapid-cooling reflective surfaces (solar glare on water, metal factory roofs, or sunbaked rocks) appear hot for an hour and vanish. Real fires smolder or spread across multiple consecutive frames. Comparing historical MODIS/VIIRS detections against co-registered Himawari thermal timelines automatically purges transient non-fire anomalies.3. Historical NLP Ground-Truth AttributionArchive Scraping & Entity Matching: The text pipeline queries digitized regional newspapers, disaster management bulletins, and agricultural department archives matching the spatio-temporal bounding box of detected hotspots.Semantic Tagging: Named Entity Recognition (NER) models extract event dates, administrative units, and cause keywords. The system flags the historical cluster with definitive labels:Agricultural Stubble Burning (e.g., synchronized post-harvest clearance)Uncontrolled Wildfire / Peatland FireIndustrial False Anomaly (e.g., refinery flares)4. Spatiotemporal Prediction & Forecasting ModuleCyclical Feature Extraction: The calendar aggregates 25 years of standardized fire events into a matrix indexed by week-of-year ($W_1$ to $W_{52}$) and geographical grid cell ($0.1^\circ \times 0.1^\circ$).Predictive Modeling: A spatio-temporal forecasting model (such as a ConvLSTM or gradient-boosted spatial time-series model like XGBoost) combines the historical calendar's recurrence periodicity with meteorological covariates (soil moisture, vapor pressure deficit, dry-spell duration). By tracking the statistical start-of-season week over 25 years without sensor bias, scientists can directly correlate fire season shifts with regional climate variables.Accurate Greenhouse Gas & Aerosol Inversion: Atmospheric modelers calculate emissions by multiplying burned area or Fire Radiative Power (FRP) by emission factors. Raw, unharmonized datasets distort emission trends over time; a standardized time-series produces accurate historical emissions baselines for carbon accounting.Evaluating Land Management Policies: Governments often pass bans on seasonal open burning (e.g., residue burning regulations). The calendar enables before-and-after policy evaluation within custom conservation zones to test whether total fire frequency actually decreased or simply shifted in timing.How the Calendar Predicts Future Fire ActivityThe calendar moves beyond static history by acting as the foundational training matrix for predictive models:Identifying Recurrent Phenological Windows: Agricultural fires follow strict agronomic cycles dictated by harvest dates and monsoon rains. Wildfires follow dry fuel buildup intervals. The calendar calculates the historical Return Interval Probability for every week of the year within each localized micro-basin.Detecting Early-Phase Trajectory Deviations: If the first two weeks of an emerging dry season record $z$-scores significantly above the 25-year baseline, the forecasting model elevates the cumulative risk profile for downstream weeks.Spatial Risk Propagation: By examining historical spatial propagation trajectories (e.g., how fires historically travel along a drying river delta or forest edge over consecutive weeks), the system projects the most probable adjacent grid cells to ignite next.The User Interface & ExperienceAOI Selector: The user opens the web application and uploads a GeoJSON file, chooses an administrative boundary, or draws a freehand polygon on a world map.Harmonized Activity View: The dashboard instantly generates a 52-week $\times$ 25-year matrix. Color gradients represent harmonized Fire Radiative Power (FRP) and cluster counts.Anomaly Inspection & Ground Truth: Clicking on any anomalous block (e.g., Week 16, 2018) opens a side drawer displaying the satellite cluster metadata, sensor agreement status (MODIS + VIIRS + Himawari), and the linked local news excerpt confirming the physical incident.Predictive Horizon Tab: Switching to "Forecast Mode" renders a forward-looking risk heatmap projecting the probability, anticipated peak calendar week, and expected severity of upcoming fire activity over the next 30 to 60 days.
